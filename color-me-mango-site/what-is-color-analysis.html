Certified Color Analysis, Explained

What is color analysis, really?

A simple breakdown of the method, the seasons, and how it all works from home.

The Simple Version

It's science, not guesswork.

Everyone has a unique mix of natural coloring. That includes your undertone (a subtle warmth or coolness beneath the skin), your chroma (how bright or soft your coloring is), the contrast between your skin, hair, and eyes, and a few other key qualities. Personal color analysis looks at all of these together, then matches you to a family of colors that enhances your natural coloring rather than competing with it.

The result is a personalized palette: dozens of specific, wearable shades that make your skin look clear and healthy, your eyes brighter, and your whole look more polished — often without changing anything else.

How I Find Your Colors

Everything starts with your photos. My photo guide shows you exactly how to take them at home, with the right light and setup, so your true coloring comes through. From there, I study your photos using established color theory, built around hue, value, and chroma, the same three qualities used to describe every color. I look at how your skin, hair, and eyes measure up on each one, and how they respond to different colors. Some colors lift and brighten you; others flatten or wash you out. The result isn't a guess or a matter of taste. It's a palette grounded in color science.

Your Photo Matters

The secret ingredient? A great photo.

Your photo is the most important part of your analysis. The wrong lighting or a colorful background can completely change how your skin, hair, and eyes look, and without the right photo, I can't do your analysis at all. The good news? It's easy once you know the tricks.

Go Bare-Faced

Remove all makeup and jewelry. Your face should be completely free of all products.

Keep It Plain Behind You

Stand in front of a white or light-colored plain background. Color in the background can actually change how your natural coloring looks.

Chase the Daylight

Take your photo in natural light, outside or near a window. Face the window when you take the shot.

Keep It Simple Up Top

Bare shoulders or a plain white shirt or top work best.

Skip the Flash

No flash, please! You don't want any shadows on your face.

Show Your Roots

If your hair is colored, include a photo with your hair covered by a white scarf or pulled all the way back, showing only your natural roots.

Bonus

Have an old photo that shows your natural hair color? Send it along. It really helps!

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can color analysis really be done from photos?

Yes! Being in person isn't necessary to find your color season. With the right photos, a digital color analysis lets me carefully study your coloring without the time limits of an appointment. You'll receive a simple photo guide to make sure lighting and color come through as accurately as possible. In-person draping is a great tool for seeing what looks best on us, but color analysis is based on science, so you don't need to be in the same room.

Do I need to meet with you live?

No. Everything happens through your questionnaire and photos, on your own schedule. If you'd love to talk live, the Peach package includes a 30-minute Zoom call with me.

How long until I get my results?

Within 5 business days of when I receive your photos. I can't start until your photos are in, so your timeframe begins the moment I have them. Need them faster? Add a 24-hour rush to the Mango package for $50, weekends included.

How do I take my photos?

After you book, you'll receive my photo guide. It walks you through lighting, setup, and what to wear, step by step, so there's no guesswork. Want a sneak peek? Check out my photo tips above.

What if my hair is dyed?

Totally fine! I'll ask about your natural hair color in your questionnaire and focus on the features that help me understand your natural coloring. If you have older photos showing your natural hair, those can be helpful as well.

What if I already think I know my season?

Tell me! I love knowing what you've been typed as before or what season you suspect you are. I won't let it determine my result, though. I'll analyze your coloring independently and explain what led me to your final season.

What happens if I'm between two seasons?

That's exactly where having a trained analyst helps. Not everyone fits perfectly into an obvious box. I'll compare neighboring seasons and look at the overall balance of your coloring before deciding which palette supports you best. I'll also show you your sister season, which you can pull from now and then.

Can my color season change?

No, your true season does not change. Your natural coloring can shift somewhat with age, changes in hair color, tanning, or other factors, but the underlying qualities that determine your palette tend to stay consistent. Your guide is designed to give you a foundation you can keep using over time.

Do I have to get rid of everything that isn't in my palette?

Absolutely not. Color analysis isn't about giving you a list of colors you're "allowed" to wear. It's about understanding which colors naturally highlight you. But I promise, once you know your colors, you won't want to go back!

Can children get their color analysis done?

Absolutely! Children ages 7 and up can have their colors analyzed, just like adults. It can be a huge help for parents, too. Once you know your child's palette, it's easy to build a closet full of pieces that work together, so they can throw on an outfit and automatically look great.

Can I give a color analysis as a gift?

Yes! Any package can be given as a gift. Just check "This is a gift" when you book and add the recipient's name and email. I'll send the welcome email and photo guide straight to them, and their timeframe starts once I receive their photos.

Can I just use ChatGPT or AI to determine my color season?

Unfortunately, ChatGPT and other AI platforms cannot accurately see or read undertones or understand colorimetry. They tend to analyze a photograph as a whole, not the individual in the photograph itself. Because of this, people are often misclassified or put into categories that make no sense for their specific contrast, chroma, and coloring.
